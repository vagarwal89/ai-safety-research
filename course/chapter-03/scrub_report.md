## What this means

A scrubber is measured by recall: what fraction of the personal data did it actually remove. On this test every expected span was removed, but the types were not always right, and the detector also removed things that were not personal data at all. Three lessons follow from the actual run.

First, indirect identifiers are caught only by accident. The card ending 1761 was removed, but only because it looked like a date. The age plus role plus city combination was broken up, but only because its pieces looked like a date and a name. If the shapes had been slightly different, both would have survived. A detector that removes something for the wrong reason is not a reliable control.

Second, scrubbing damages text. The word 'Email' at the start of a sentence was tagged as a person, 'UPI' was tagged as a nationality, and 'Meet Sara' was swallowed whole into one person entity. Every false positive deletes real meaning from the training data. Recall and precision pull in opposite directions, and the right balance depends on what the data is for.

Third, add custom recognizers for the identifier shapes your data actually contains. The UPI id and the employee id were both found with the right type only because this script adds recognizers for them. A US-tuned default knows neither shape. That is the general rule: a scrubber is only as good as its fit to your data.

Never report a scrub as clean on the basis of the tool running. Report the measured recall, the wrong types, the false positives, and what you did about them. That record is your GDPR Article 5(1)(c) data minimization evidence and part of your Article 10(2)(c) preparation record.
