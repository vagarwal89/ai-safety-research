## What this means

The first merges are the most common letter pairs in the corpus, usually pieces of the most common words: 'th', 'he', 'ing', 'ed'. That is the whole trick of BPE. Frequent patterns get short codes, rare words stay long. The third test sentence shows the safety net: 'xylophone' never appears in TinyStories, so the tokenizer falls back to small pieces and single letters instead of failing. No word is ever unrepresentable.

Tokens per word is the number that matters downstream. A tokenizer trained on simple stories will split technical or non-English text into many more pieces, which costs context window and compute. That is why production models train their tokenizer on the same kind of data they will train on, and why the tokenizer is part of the preparation record, not an afterthought.
